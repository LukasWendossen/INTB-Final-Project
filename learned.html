What I learned

Organizational design and control (Ch 10)

  • What you learned: how multinationals choose a structure and use control mechanisms (output, bureaucratic, cultural, personal) to keep subsidiaries aligned with headquarters.
  • Why it was meaningful: it named your problem. You had goals (strategy) but no structure or controls to carry them out.
  • How it changed your understanding: success is designed, not just willed. Go beyond defining the terms.

Strategy and its execution (Ch 9)

  • Strategy is a choice about how to compete and where to focus. Connect it to choosing priorities across three courses.
  • Optional: global integration vs. local responsiveness, like one central system with each course's own rules (AI policies, platforms, exam booking).

Communicating across contexts (Hall and Trompenaars)

  • From your Cultural Environment Analysis (Japan vs. Italy, graded 94): high-context vs. low-context communication, and Trompenaars' dimensions.
  • What it taught you about professional communication: reading the context, being clear and direct when it counts, like preparing for the meeting with Prof. Wengelnik.
  • How it applies professionally: working with people across cultures in a global firm.
